Business Insights


1. Loan Portfolio Performance


The analysis of 255,347 loan applications revealed an overall loan default rate of 11.61%, indicating that the majority of borrowers successfully met their repayment obligations. This reflects a relatively stable loan portfolio while emphasizing the need for effective credit risk management.


2. Credit Score as a Primary Risk Indicator


Borrowers with lower credit scores demonstrated a significantly higher likelihood of default compared to those with higher credit scores. This highlights credit score as one of the most influential factors in evaluating borrower creditworthiness and predicting repayment behavior.


3. Income and Repayment Capacity


The analysis showed that borrowers who defaulted had a lower average annual income ($71.84K) than non-defaulters ($83.90K). This suggests that income level plays an important role in determining a borrower's ability to meet loan repayment obligations.


4. Interest Rate and Default Risk


Defaulters were associated with a higher average interest rate (15.9%) compared to non-defaulters (13.2%). Higher borrowing costs may increase financial pressure on borrowers, raising the probability of default.


5. Loan Amount Analysis


Contrary to common assumptions, non-defaulters received a higher average loan amount ($144.52K) than defaulters ($125.35K). This indicates that loan amount alone is not a reliable predictor of default, and borrower financial strength should be considered alongside loan size.


6. Borrower Demographics


Employment type, age group, education level, and marital status exhibited varying default patterns. However, these demographic characteristics should be evaluated together with financial indicators rather than used independently when assessing credit risk.


7. Loan Purpose


Different loan purposes showed variations in default counts, suggesting that certain categories of loans may carry greater repayment risk. Financial institutions can use this information to strengthen loan evaluation strategies for higher-risk loan categories.


8. Mortgage Status


The analysis indicated relatively similar default patterns between borrowers with and without mortgages. This suggests that mortgage ownership alone is not a significant determinant of loan default within this dataset.


Business Recommendations


Based on the analysis, the following recommendations are proposed:


Prioritize credit score and borrower income during the loan approval process.
Implement risk-based pricing by carefully evaluating borrowers with low credit scores and higher interest rates.
Combine financial indicators (income, credit score, interest rate) with borrower demographics to improve credit risk assessment.
Continuously monitor high-risk borrower segments to reduce future loan defaults.
Develop predictive risk models to support faster and more accurate lending decisions.

Project Conclusion


This project demonstrates how MySQL and Power BI can be used to analyze loan default data and uncover meaningful business insights. The findings indicate that credit score, income, and interest rate are the most influential factors associated with loan default, while demographic characteristics provide additional context when combined with financial metrics. These insights can help financial institutions enhance credit risk assessment, optimize lending strategies, and improve portfolio performance.
